RULES — these are hiring-intent semantics, not search mechanics. Two principles govern everything below: NEVER LOSE a stated requirement or option, and NEVER INVENT one the source does not support. You may normalize wording; you may not manufacture or drop meaning.

1. ROLE ARCHETYPE. Classify how the role is defined:
   - "title_defined": the title itself carries strong occupational meaning (e.g. Product Owner, Recruiter, Accountant). The title family will drive the search.
   - "skill_defined": the title is generic and a technology/discipline defines the person (e.g. "Software Engineer" who must know Python). The skills define the search.
   - "hybrid": both matter (e.g. "AI Product Manager").
   Give a confidence in [0,1] and a ONE-SENTENCE rationale. Do not write a long explanation.

2. ROLE FAMILY — the role(s) the recruiter ACTUALLY expressed, normalized only for wording. Do NOT expand to a broader search family: that is the compiler's job, not yours. Include only titles the source states or is clearly the same role. Strip seniority into the seniority field (so "Senior Product Owner" -> role_family ["Product Owner"], seniority "Senior"). Do NOT add adjacent titles, variants, or a broad engineering/discipline family the source did not state. Examples: a brief for "Software Engineers who know Python" -> role_family ["Software Engineer"] (NOT Backend Engineer, Python Developer, etc.). A "Senior Product Owner" brief -> role_family ["Product Owner"]. Principle: extraction preserves meaning; the compiler expands representation.

3. REQUIRED vs PREFERRED — strength is under-sensitive by default; correct for that.
   - Mark "required" when the source presents the signal as a qualification the candidate is expected to possess. This includes explicit mandatory language ("must", "required", a stated minimum, "only candidates who...") AND strong qualification language: "proficient in", "strong experience with", "extensive experience in", "hands-on experience with", "expertise in", and equivalent formulations. A plainly stated qualification ("experience with X") is required when presented as an expectation.
   - Mark "preferred" ONLY when the source explicitly weakens it: "preferred", "preferably", "nice to have", "a plus", "bonus", "ideally", or equivalent.
   - Do NOT infer "preferred" merely because the source does not literally say "required".
   Examples: "proficient in Python" -> required. "extensive experience with Java" -> required. "experience with Python" (as a qualification) -> required. "Python is a plus" -> preferred. "ideally FastAPI" -> preferred.

4. TEMPORAL RELATIONSHIP — apply to skills, companies, and company_scale. Determine it from the language surrounding the signal, not from the mere fact that the term appears.
   - "current": "currently", "presently", "now", or the present-tense technical stack of the role.
   - "past": "previously", "earlier", "earlier in career", "has been", "worked on ... earlier".
   - "any": only when the source gives NO temporal evidence.
   NEVER output "any" when the source provides enough evidence to choose "current" or "past". Example: "presently working on Python with Django/FastAPI, worked extensively on Java earlier" -> Python current, Django current, FastAPI current, Java past.

5. OPTION CONSERVATION (two-level logic only). When the source presents alternatives in an OR/AND group, preserve EVERY semantically distinct stated option. Never silently drop one to simplify output or because another seems more common.
   - "B.Tech / B.E / M.Tech" -> all three degrees. "CS, IT, Data Science or AI" -> all four streams.
   - Independent skills go in "skills". "X and/or Y" / "X or Y" goes in "skill_any_of" as an OR group preserving both. A "skill_any_of" group MUST contain 2 or more alternatives — never a single skill (a lone skill always goes in "skills"). Do NOT build nested logic beyond this.

6. COMPANIES. A named company the recruiter likes is "preferred" unless the source clearly says the candidate must currently/previously work there. Never assume a listed company is mandatory. Capture every named company and its relationship (current/past/any) faithfully.

7. EDUCATION. List the EXACT degrees and streams stated — never broaden and never drop (see Option Conservation). Degrees are an OR set; streams are an OR set.

8. LOCATION. Multiple places ("Hyderabad or Pune") are OR entries. Use "City, State, Country" form where known. When a commute radius is stated ("within 25 miles of Hyderabad", "25 mi radius"), ALSO emit location.radius {value, unit, around} with the anchor place — a radius is different from a plain city match, so capture it only when actually stated.

8b. EXCLUSIONS are explicit and are NOT the inverse of a required include. "Don't show / exclude people currently at X" -> exclusion kind exclude_current_company; "...who ever worked at X" -> exclude_any_company; "...previously at X" -> exclude_past_company; "exclude <title>" -> exclude_title. A company the recruiter LIKES goes in companies (include/prefer); a company to screen OUT goes in exclusions.

9. SKILLS vs EVIDENCE SIGNALS — a sharp boundary.
   - A "skill" is a named technical/professional COMPETENCY that can be searched for as a candidate attribute: Python, Java, FastAPI, Django, Kubernetes, SQL, Machine Learning.
   - An "evidence signal" is a CAPABILITY, RESPONSIBILITY, OWNERSHIP, BEHAVIOR, or OUTCOME that must be demonstrated through a candidate's record: product vision, backlog ownership, stakeholder management, Agile delivery, owning product strategy, cross-functional leadership, AI fluency.
   - Do NOT convert a capability/responsibility into a skill merely because it can be written as a noun phrase ("backlog ownership" is NOT a skill).
   - A named methodology or domain may be a skill when the source clearly treats it as one; otherwise prefer evidence_signal for capability statements.

10. NEVER output provider or database terms (no field names, no operators, no "filter"/"hard"/"relevance"/"retrieval"). If you are tempted to say how to search, stop — describe the meaning instead.

11. SOURCE ROLES AND PRIORITY. The two sources have different roles; do not merge them as equal flat requirements, and do not treat the brief as universally higher.
   - The JOB DESCRIPTION is authoritative for formal role responsibilities, formal qualifications, stated experience and skill requirements, formal preferred qualifications, and the role description.
   - The RECRUITER / HIRING-MANAGER BRIEF is authoritative for SOURCING INTERPRETATION where it explicitly clarifies, narrows, waives, or corrects the JD; where it adds sourcing constraints the JD does not state; where it defines hard negatives; and where it defines alternative sourcing paths.
   - A JD requirement the brief does not address is RETAINED at the strength the JD states.
   - Where the sources genuinely conflict and the brief does not clearly resolve it, do NOT silently pick one: add a reconciliation with action "unresolved".

12. RECONCILIATIONS. When the brief narrows, waives, or contradicts a JD item, that JD item must not survive as a positive requirement at its original scope or strength. Record the decision in "reconciliations": topic, action (narrowed = brief limits the JD item; waived = brief says the JD item is not required, for everyone or for one path; contradicted = brief says the JD item does not apply; unresolved = conflict the brief does not settle), jd_quote and brief_quote (verbatim), result (one sentence: what the intent now says), path_id (the path it applies to, or null for global). Do not record a reconciliation for something the brief merely repeats or adds.
   Illustration from an unrelated role: JD asks for "experience with fraud monitoring"; brief says "this is a payments-data role, not fraud operations" -> do not keep fraud monitoring as a requirement; record it as contradicted, and carry the not-fraud-operations negative in semantic_exclusions.

13. SOURCING PATHS. Emit "sourcing_paths" ONLY when the source describes genuinely alternative, legitimate ways to find an acceptable candidate, each with its own conditions. Otherwise leave it empty. Each path has an id, a label, and a strategy:
   - "domain_led": what identifies the candidate is experience in a particular domain / problem space; other requirements may be relaxed.
   - "capability_led": what identifies the candidate is skills, tools and level, whatever domain they worked in.
   - "hybrid": both matter.
   Put in the global fields what holds for EVERY path. Put in a path ONLY what differs for that path. A path's seniority, experience or location REPLACES the global value for that path; a path skill or domain entry with the same name REPLACES the global entry for that path; any other path entry is an addition. Do not copy the global intent into the paths. If a requirement is stated for only some paths, it belongs to those paths, not to the global fields. Never merge two alternative paths into one list of requirements. Do not invent a path.
   Illustration from an unrelated role: a "claims adjuster" brief with "track 1: experience in marine claims, anywhere in Brazil; track 2: any senior adjuster in Sao Paulo, marine experience not needed" -> two paths, each with its own location; marine claims is a domain on the first and not a requirement on the second.

14. PROFICIENCY. Set "proficiency" on a skill when the source distinguishes depth: "hands_on" (the candidate is expected to use it directly and substantively) or "working_knowledge" (a lower bar: working familiarity). Use null when no depth is stated. Proficiency is separate from strength: strength says how much the skill is wanted; proficiency says how deeply. Never encode depth into strength.

15. LEADERSHIP. When the level word implies leadership, "seniority.leadership" lists which kinds of leadership the source accepts: "people" (managing, mentoring or coordinating others) and/or "technical" (technical direction, ownership, standard-setting). List both when either suffices. Do not assume people management from a leadership word. Leave the list empty when the source is silent.

16. SEMANTIC EXCLUSIONS. A kind of work or background the sources say the target is NOT, or that must be screened out, goes in "semantic_exclusions" as meaning: a short concept plus "includes", the examples the source names. It is not a company or a title unless the source names a specific company or title. Never turn a work type into a company-name exclusion. "exclusions" stays for named companies and titles only.

17. DOMAIN. "domain" holds the work domain, problem space or environment a candidate's evidence should show. It is not a skill and not a company: never turn a domain, background or environment into a company requirement. Strength follows the same required / preferred rules; use "context" when the domain only describes the team or setting.

18. LOCATION LEVEL. Use the narrowest form the source states. A country-wide area is the country name alone, with no cities added; a city is "City, State, Country". Never turn a country into a city, never add places the source does not name.

19. PROVENANCE. Every atom carries basis.sources: "jd" (the JD states it), "recruiter_brief" (the brief states it; list both when both do), "approved_knowledge" (only when a maintained taxonomy supplies it and neither source states it), or "inferred" (your own inference: it must never be "required"). basis.quote is a SHORT verbatim excerpt (at most 200 characters) copied exactly from the cited source text, or null when inferred. Never invent a quote and never give line numbers. Your claims are checked against the source text by code; an unsupported claim is reported.
