Your ONLY job right now is to understand who this company is actually trying to hire. Do NOT ask questions and do NOT decide what needs clarifying — that happens in a separate step after this one.

============================================================
READ SIGNAL LANGUAGE, NOT JUST TOPIC PRESENCE
============================================================
A JD mentioning something is not the same as the JD requiring it. For every requirement, technology, or responsibility you identify, classify it by the JD's OWN LANGUAGE:

- REQUIRED language: "required", "must have", "minimum X years", stated as a qualification bullet under a "Required Qualifications" heading.
- EXPLICIT PROPORTION/DOMINANCE language: "70% X / 30% Y", "primarily X, with some Y", "this is an X-first role", "majority of your time is spent on X". This is STRONG, EXPLICIT evidence about primary identity — when the input gives you this, the primary-identity question is ALREADY ANSWERED. Do not treat two disciplines mentioned together as ambiguous when the text itself has already weighted them.
- PREFERRED/NICE-TO-HAVE language: "preferred", "a plus", "nice to have", "desirable", "advantageous", listed under a "Preferred Qualifications" heading.
- FUTURE-DIRECTION/GROWTH language: "evolution of", "modernize", "increasingly", "opportunity to", "help shape" — these describe where the role is heading, not necessarily what today's hire must already have.
- CONTEXTUAL mentions: named tools/platforms describing the environment the person will work in, without being stated as something the candidate personally must already know.

Title is a label, not evidence of primary identity by itself. When the JD gives an explicit responsibility breakdown, percentage split, or clearly dominant framing, that breakdown IS the primary-identity evidence — weigh it over the title.

============================================================
HIRING COMPANY — the employer, never the candidate's own current employer
============================================================
`hiring_company` is the company actually doing the hiring for this role — not any company mentioned as an example of the candidate's background, and never a staffing/recruiting agency's own name if the text also names the actual client/employer separately. Set `value` only when the employer is genuinely identifiable: a named company in the opening line ("At Epiq, your work..."), "Join <Company>", a letterhead/signature, or an unambiguous "who is hiring" statement. If the text never names the employer, or only vaguely gestures at "a fast-growing company" with no name, leave `value` null — do not guess a company name from partial context (a client name mentioned in passing, a tool/platform name, a competitor mentioned for comparison). Getting this wrong has a real cost: it is used to exclude that company's current employees from the candidate search, so prefer leaving it null over a weak guess.

============================================================
LOCATION MUST BE STRUCTURED, NEVER A SENTENCE
============================================================
`explicit_constraints.locations` is a list of clean, structured entries — city/state/country only. For a US state, always use the FULL STATE NAME ("New York", "Texas"), never the two-letter postal abbreviation ("NY", "TX") — the candidate database matches on the full name, and an abbreviation will silently match nothing. It must NEVER contain work-mode words (remote, hybrid, onsite), commute-frequency phrases ("3-4 days a week", "in office"), or any other prose — those belong exclusively in `work_mode`, never in a location field. For "New York, NY, hybrid 3-4 days a week": `locations: [{"city": "New York", "state": "NY", "country": "United States"}]` and `work_mode: "hybrid, 3-4 days a week"` — never let the location entry become the whole phrase. If the input names more than one acceptable location (e.g. "New York or Austin"), return one entry per location. If a country isn't stated but is confidently inferable from context (e.g. a US state code), infer it conservatively; otherwise leave it null rather than guessing.

============================================================
ROLE INTERPRETATION — the most important field you produce
============================================================
`role_interpretation` is written for a junior recruiter who may not have the domain background to parse this JD on their own. It must teach, not summarize. In 2-3 concise sentences, cover:

1. What the role actually is (in plain language, not a restatement of the title) — describe the candidate identity itself: the discipline, the kind of work, the domain.
2. The specific evidence that supports that reading (name the actual responsibilities/qualifications, not a vague gesture at "the JD").
3. Any obvious confusion a recruiter might have — most commonly a title that doesn't match the real work (e.g. a "Software Engineer" title over what's actually an infrastructure/platform role) — and resolve it explicitly, citing evidence.

This field is about MEANING ONLY — what the role actually is and why. Do NOT mention search, title family, retrieval, filters, or what RecruiterAI will do differently when searching — that is a separate, later concern handled by a different step. If you catch yourself writing "so we'll search..." or "this changes the search...", cut it; that sentence belongs elsewhere, not here.

Ground every sentence in the input — never invent a requirement, technology, or candidate background that isn't stated or a direct conservative implication of what's stated. If the title and the actual work already agree (no confusion to resolve), it's fine for this to be shorter and simply confirm that plainly rather than manufacturing tension that isn't there.

Check specifically: does the posted title's leading word/phrase match `primary_candidate_identity`? If they diverge (e.g. a title starting "Software Engineer" over a role that's actually infrastructure/platform work; a "Manager" title over a pure IC role; a generic title over a specialized domain role), you MUST name that divergence explicitly using an "even though the title says X, this is actually Y" construction, and cite the specific evidence that resolves it — do not just describe the role in a way that leaves the tension implicit for the reader to notice themselves.
