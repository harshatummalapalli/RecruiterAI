RULES — these are hiring-intent semantics, not search mechanics:

1. ROLE ARCHETYPE. Classify how the role is defined:
   - "title_defined": the title itself carries strong occupational meaning (e.g. Product Owner, Recruiter, Accountant). The title family will drive the search.
   - "skill_defined": the title is generic and a technology/discipline defines the person (e.g. "Software Engineer" who must know Python). The skills define the search.
   - "hybrid": both matter (e.g. "AI Product Manager").
   Give a confidence in [0,1] and a ONE-SENTENCE rationale. Do not write a long explanation.

2. ROLE FAMILY. The set of plausible job titles that mean the same role, broad enough to catch real people. For a skill_defined role, use the broad engineering/discipline family (e.g. Software Engineer, Backend Engineer, Backend Developer), NOT the skill as a title.

3. REQUIRED vs PREFERRED — never upgrade a preference into a requirement.
   - "required": explicitly mandatory ("must", "required", a stated minimum, "only candidates who...").
   - "preferred": "preferably", "nice to have", "a plus", "ideally", "bonus".
   - "context": descriptive flavor that shouldn't gate anyone.
   Example: "Python and preferably FastAPI" -> Python required, FastAPI preferred. "Java is a plus" -> Java preferred.

4. TEMPORAL RELATIONSHIP — apply to skills, companies, and company_scale.
   - "past": "previously", "earlier in career", "has been", "worked on ... earlier".
   - "current": "currently", "presently", "now".
   - "any": unspecified, or "at some point".
   "Currently works at a 5000+ company" and "has worked at an enterprise" are DIFFERENT — encode the relationship faithfully.

5. SKILL GROUPING (two-level logic only).
   - Independent required/preferred skills go in "skills".
   - "X and/or Y" or "X or Y" goes in "skill_any_of" as an OR group.
   - Do NOT build nested logic beyond this.

6. COMPANIES. A named company the recruiter likes is usually "preferred" unless the brief clearly says the candidate must currently/previously work there. Never assume a listed company is mandatory. Capture relationship (current/past/any) faithfully.

7. EDUCATION. List the EXACT degrees and streams stated — never broaden them. "B.Tech or B.E in CS, IT, Data Science or AI" -> degrees:[B.Tech,B.E], streams:[Computer Science, Information Technology, Data Science, Artificial Intelligence]. Degrees are an OR set; streams are an OR set.

8. LOCATION. Multiple places ("Hyderabad or Pune") are OR entries. Use "City, State, Country" form where known.

9. EVIDENCE SIGNALS. Capability/narrative requirements that describe what a good candidate demonstrates (e.g. "product vision", "backlog ownership", "microservices", "stakeholder engagement"). These are things to verify in a candidate's record — carry their strength, but they are NOT structural filters.
